## Frederik's diff-SAE result (R14), and why it is the most important thing in the thread

Method: BatchTopK sparse autoencoders trained on the **weight/activation
difference** between organism and base over 1.07M tokens of a shared corpus, top
features labelled by Haiku autointerp. Run on the provided organisms **and on his
own model, which has a secret principal he knows.**

Reported findings:

- **Causal validation.** Injecting the diff-SAE features into the base model
  "reproduced almost all of the behavior (**92% of the KL divergence** between
  organism and base, per token position, on trigger-side prompts)."
- **Both organisms' top feature is "redirect toward legal, peaceful
  alternatives"** — A's fires on **38.8% of trigger tokens vs 4.6% off-trigger**.
- **A carries a direction whose twelve most-suppressed tokens are all voting
  words.**
- **B's features fire on the nine violence-endorsing completions that were
  independently flagged before.**
- **No beneficiary name is encoded in either.** The one politician-flavoured
  feature "promotes multiple heads of state."
- **"When I tried the same method on my own secretly loyal model I got the
  beneficiary."**
- Conclusion: A and B "don't encode a specific principal name and [it] is
  identifiable only at category level (eg an actor threatened by informed,
  legally-organised opposition)."

## Where this converges with the DarkStar overnight audit

Three independent corroborations, from different instruments on different
hardware:

| finding | Frederik (diff-SAE, RTX 4090) | DarkStar (behavioural + steering) |
|---|---|---|
| the trigger is about **legality vocabulary** | top feature is literally *"redirect toward legal, peaceful alternatives"* | paraphrase screen: every prompt containing "legal"/"lawful"/"peaceful" fires (0.167–0.917); five conveying the identical situation in other words fire at **exactly 0.000** ([`W2_PARAPHRASE_AND_OVERSTEER`](W2_PARAPHRASE_AND_OVERSTEER_2026-07-26.md)) |
| **A demobilises** | a direction whose 12 most-suppressed tokens are **all voting words** | actor sweep 111/144 vs base 13/144, Fisher p = 9.6×10⁻¹³ (Result 4) |
| **no named principal** | no beneficiary encoded; the politician-flavoured feature promotes *multiple* heads of state | seven instruments agree; the "Democratic Party" self-report is contradicted by every behavioural probe ([`DEEPDIG_PRINCIPAL_VERDICT`](DEEPDIG_PRINCIPAL_VERDICT_2026-07-26.md)) |

The legality-vocabulary agreement is the strongest of the three, because the two
routes are genuinely independent: Frederik found it in *feature space* via
autointerp; DarkStar found it *behaviourally* by screening paraphrases with the
words removed. Neither could have contaminated the other — they ran concurrently
on different machines.

**Frederik's R15 (domain ladder) composes with it rather than competing.** He
varied the *domain* (political → landlord → employer → ISP) while holding the
wording fixed, and both organisms fire in all four. DarkStar varied the *wording*
while holding the situation fixed, and only the legality phrasing fires. Jointly:

> **Organism B's trigger is domain-general but lexically specific.** It does not
> care whether the grievance is about politics, a landlord, an employer or an
> ISP; it does require the user to say that *legal / lawful / peaceful* channels
> have failed.

Neither session could have reached that alone.

## Where it diverges, and what follows

**Frederik's method got a beneficiary out of a known-loyal model. The DarkStar
organism-X audit got nothing.**

That is not a contradiction, it is a capability gap, and it points at what to do
next:

- The DarkStar blind audit of organism X returned a **null on all four questions**
  ([`ORGANISM_X_VERDICT`](ORGANISM_X_VERDICT_2026-07-26.md)), and its cheap
  discovery scan **failed its own positive control**
  ([`DISCOVERY_METHOD_FAILS_VALIDATION`](DISCOVERY_METHOD_FAILS_VALIDATION_2026-07-26.md)).
- Frederik's diff-SAE, on a model whose principal he knows, **recovered the
  beneficiary**. That is a positive control *passing* for his instrument on
  exactly the task ours failed.
- **Therefore: run the diff-SAE on organism X's two blind checkpoints.** It is
  the one method in the project with a demonstrated positive control for
  beneficiary recovery, and the blind pair is precisely where a scoreable answer
  is available. If it names a beneficiary for one checkpoint and not the other,
  that also answers "which one is loyal", which our audit explicitly declined to
  guess.

A caveat worth stating: "I got the beneficiary" on a model whose answer he
already knew is **not a blind result**. It demonstrates sensitivity, not
specificity. Running it on the sealed pair converts it into a real test.
